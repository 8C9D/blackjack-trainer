### How this ships

Two independent channels, neither of which is a server:

1. **Web: a static site on GitHub Pages.** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds with `--base-href /blackjack-trainer/`, assembles `site/` from `dist/blackjack-trainer/browser/` plus the two legal pages plus a copy of the shell as `404.html`, and deploys via `actions/deploy-pages@v4` to `https://8c9d.github.io/blackjack-trainer/`.
2. **iOS: an App Store binary.** A paid iPhone app, `MARKETING_VERSION 1.0`, bundle id `com.arthurzhang.blackjacktrainer.app`, signed with `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM C3W798H8U8` (`ios/project.yml:20`). Submission is manual and owner-driven (`LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md`).

There is no container, no server binary, and no published library.

### What "production" means here, and who breaks

Derived from `README.md`, `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, `LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md` and `ios/AppStore/*.html`:

- **The user who breaks is a trainee**, and the thing they lose is practice history - streaks, accuracy, weak spots - held only in `localStorage` (web) or `UserDefaults`/iCloud KVS (iOS). There is no server copy and no account. `src/app/core/services/storage.ts:44-52` says this in the source itself.
- **The second party who breaks is the owner**, a single developer shipping a paid App Store binary and a public GitHub Pages site under their own name. A false published privacy statement or a broken store URL lands on them personally.
- There is no wagering, no money, no PII, no telemetry, and no third party receiving anything. That materially lowers the ceiling on how bad any defect here can be, and the severities below reflect that.

## NOT DEFECTS

- **`tools/serve-dist.mjs:40`'s path-traversal claim is true.** The comment asserts `normalize()` prevents escaping `ROOT`. Verified by execution: `/%2e%2e/%2e%2e/%2e%2e/package.json` → 404, and `--path-as-is /../../package.json` → 404. `new URL(...).pathname` always begins with `/`, and `normalize` collapses leading `..` on an absolute path. No finding.
- **The App Store privacy text is accurate as shipped.** `ios/AppStore/privacy.html:48-50` and `support.html:45` say "This version of the app does not sync", while `AppModel.swift:49` does construct a real `UbiquitousKeyValueStore`. With the capability unprovisioned (assumption 5), `NSUbiquitousKeyValueStore` behaves as a local store and no data leaves the device, so the statement holds. Recorded because the statement's truth depends on an out-of-repo portal setting rather than on anything in this tree - a coupling worth knowing about, but not a code defect.
- **`ios/project.yml:20` `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: C3W798H8U8` is not a secret.** An Apple Team ID is public in every distributed binary's provisioning profile and grants nothing on its own.
- **`ios/BlackjackTrainer/App/AppModel.swift:95` `preconditionFailure` on bundled-data load failure** is a deliberate fail-loud on a build-integrity invariant that CI gates (`ios/BlackjackTrainerTests`, plus the parity fixture check). Crashing on a corrupt build beats running with silently wrong strategy charts in a trainer whose entire value is being right.
- **`BackupService.restore` on a valid but empty backup wipes local data** (`src/app/core/services/backup.service.ts:83-126`). That is the documented semantic - a restore is the state the backup captured, not a merge - and `build()` legitimately produces `data: {}` on a fresh browser. Correct as written.
